# Mali's Centre Cannot Hold: An Analysis
**Africa Blind Spot · in-depth insight · as of 25 September 2026**

> **Bottom line:** Central Mali, the Mopti and Ségou regions between the desert north and the capital, has become the hinge of the whole war. September's clashes there are not a flare-up. They are the latest proof of a trend the Africa Center for Strategic Studies has tracked for years: **militant violence in Mali has tripled under the junta**. The insurgents have also moved from fighting for territory to **strangling the state economically**. The junta's answer, Russian firepower plus an information blackout, is feeding the insurgency it is meant to defeat.

---

## 0. Checking the claim first

**What we can confirm:**
- The Africa Center for Strategic Studies (ACSS), part of the US Defense Department, describes Mali's attacks as part of a **"long trajectory of worsening security"**, not isolated escalations.
- ACSS reports that annual deaths linked to militant Islamist groups **have tripled under the military junta**, and that the insurgent threat "exceeds the capacity of the Mali military to counter on its own".
- It frames recent attacks as an **explicit economic strategy to isolate Bamako**.

**What we couldn't find:**
- The specific ACSS item on "renewed security alerts" in the central regions. Its website is blocked from this environment.
- Any September 2026 US Embassy alert. The most recent we found are from **28 April, 30 April and 29 May 2026**: shelter-in-place advice, JNIM checkpoints on roads into Bamako, and limits on travel.

**Treat the headline as consistent with the evidence, but cite ACSS's published analysis rather than a specific "alert" until it is confirmed.**

---

## 1. Why the centre is the hinge

Central Mali is where the country's three worlds meet:
- **North:** the desert, the Tuareg separatist FLA and Kidal.
- **South:** Bamako, the government and the fuel lifelines from Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire.
- **Centre:** the **Inner Niger Delta**, farmland, cattle routes and the **Mopti–Sévaré** hub, whose airport links the army to the north.

**Whoever controls the centre controls movement between north and south.** That's why JNIM's latest blows landed there:

| Date (2026) | Place | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **9 Aug** | **San** (Ségou) | JNIM attacked a garrison: 10 soldiers killed (40 militants reported killed). |
| **10 Sept** | **Dioura** (Mopti) | The deadliest blow in years. Soldiers killed: about 120 (diplomatic source) or 130 (security source); JNIM claims 150, including two colonels. JNIM claims 93 soldiers captured; a security source says more than 100 were taken hostage. **Five Russian Africa Corps personnel** were reportedly killed. |
| **Sept** | **Sévaré and Konna** (Mopti) | JNIM claims it struck **air-defence systems at Sévaré airport** and killed 33 pro-government fighters. |
| **Sun, 13 Sept (reported 16 Sept)** | **Ke-Macina** (Ségou) | Sources say an army unit **executed 11 civilians** and threw their bodies into the river in sacks. |

**The pattern to notice:** Dioura was also hit in **2023** and **2025**. The army keeps getting attacked in the same places, which suggests JNIM is not just raiding but **systematically wearing down fixed positions** the state cannot reinforce.

## 2. Six insights

### Insight 1: This is economic war, not a war for territory
JNIM no longer needs to hold towns to win. It needs to make the state **too expensive to run**.
- **The fuel blockade:**
  - Began 3 September 2025; full blockade of Bamako declared 28 April 2026.
  - More than **300 tankers burned**.
  - Mali imports about **95%** of its fuel by road, so every convoy now needs an armed escort.
- **The countryside:** JNIM checkpoints on the roads into Bamako, and taxation of rural areas.
- **What it achieves:** It forces the army to spread thin guarding convoys, which leaves camps like Dioura exposed.

ACSS calls this the most consequential campaign against a Sahelian state in the past year. **The centre is where the strategy bites.** Every road from the coast to the north passes through or near it.

### Insight 2: The state's own violence is JNIM's best recruiter
Central Mali's insurgency grew out of **Katiba Macina**, a JNIM unit rooted among **Fulani** herders who have long felt marginalised. Much of the fighting has become ethnic:
- Fulani civilians are accused of sympathising with jihadists.
- Dogon and other militias, such as **Dan Na Ambassagou**, formed in response.
- Massacres followed on all sides: for example, **157** Fulani civilians killed at Ogossagou (2019) and **132** killed in Bankass Cercle (2022).

**In 2026, abuses by state-aligned forces continue, and they fall hardest on Fulani communities:**
- **15 June:** An Africa Corps airstrike on **Kyrnia** (Mopti) killed **8 civilians, including 3 children** (HRW).
- **10 July:** At **Bombori** (Mopti), Africa Corps and Malian soldiers rounded up 80 Fulani men and boys. They killed **9 civilians, including 4 children**; one victim was beheaded (HRW).
- **13 Sept:** At **Ke-Macina**, 11 civilians were reportedly executed by soldiers.

**The insight:** Every village punished for "collaboration" becomes a village with a reason to collaborate. In the centre, **counterinsurgency by collective punishment is a recruitment machine**, and JNIM's propaganda relies on it.

### Insight 3: The junta's security bargain is failing on its own terms
Colonel Goïta's government promised Malians security in exchange for:
- expelling the French and the UN,
- delaying elections,
- dissolving political parties,
- and giving him a new five-year term without a vote.

**The scorecard:**
- Militant-linked deaths **tripled** under the junta (ACSS). Mali has about **2,650 deaths a year**, a quarter of the Sahel's total.
- **April 2026:**
  - The biggest offensive since 2012 reached **Bamako and Kati**.
  - Defence Minister **Sadio Camara** was killed by a suicide car bomb at his home, along with his wife and two grandchildren.
  - The FLA **retook Kidal**, the junta's symbolic 2023 victory.
- **September:** More than 100 soldiers were killed at Dioura, and videos show dozens of captured soldiers.

**Why it matters politically:** A military government's legitimacy rests on military success. Losses on this scale, and soldiers paraded in enemy videos, **erode the regime's own base: the army**. Coups in Mali (2012, 2020, 2021) have repeatedly followed battlefield humiliation. *This is analysis, not a prediction, but it is the risk to watch.*

### Insight 4: Russia is reaching its limits
Russia's **Africa Corps** (formerly Wagner) was brought in as the junta's force multiplier. September exposed its limits:
- **Five personnel reportedly killed** at Dioura.
- Reports say Africa Corps forces were **cornered for more than a week** near Dioura, and a Russian drone was **brought down** by the militants as the mercenaries pulled back.
- Moscow's capacity is shaped by Ukraine. Mali gets airpower and escorts, but not the manpower to hold the countryside.

**The insight:** Airstrikes and convoy escorts can keep Bamako supplied. They cannot win back the rural centre, and their civilian toll (Insight 2) makes that harder.

### Insight 5: An odd-couple coalition that works for now
- **JNIM** (al-Qaeda-linked, seeking Islamic rule) and the **FLA** (Tuareg nationalists seeking autonomy or independence for the north) want different Malis. Since April they have **coordinated attacks** anyway.
- **Islamic State Sahel**, JNIM's rival, exploits the chaos. For example, it seized **Labbezanga** during the April offensive.
- **Analyst's view:** Control Risks' Beverly Ochieng says the JNIM–FLA alliance "poses a threat to the government's consolidation of national territory". Clashes will keep undermining any political settlement.

**The insight:** The junta now faces a **multi-front war with no single enemy to negotiate with**. Its refusal of outside mediation (it left ECOWAS and the ICC) leaves few paths to de-escalation.

### Insight 6: The blackout makes the crisis invisible
- **Journalists are being jailed:**
  - Abdramane Keïta, director of *Le Témoin*, got **two years** (14 Sept) after saying on TV that JNIM controlled a northern city.
  - In August, another prominent journalist got **seven years** for criticising the junta.
- **The numbers are probably too low:** ACSS warns that Sahel fatality counts are likely an **undercount**, because of juntas' restrictions on reporting.
- **The effect:** If saying "JNIM controls this town" is a crime, the public, partners and even the junta's own decision-makers lose an accurate picture.

**The insight:** Mali's information blackout is not just a press-freedom issue. It is a **strategic blind spot for the state itself**, and the reason the world underestimates this war.

## 3. The regional picture
- **The Sahel is Africa's deadliest theatre:** 9,928 militant-linked deaths in the past year, **42%** of Africa's total. That's nearly **49,000 since 2022** (ACSS).
- **JNIM is linked to 78% of Sahel fatalities** (ACSS) and is spreading south and west, toward the coastal states: Benin, Togo, Côte d'Ivoire and Senegal.
- **Niger:** JNIM attacked **Niamey's airport** in June 2026. The juntas' **Alliance of Sahel States (AES)** is being tested in all three capitals at once.
- **Mali's lifelines run through Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire.** Pressure on those roads is pressure on West Africa's economy.

---

## 4. Scenarios, next 3–6 months (analysis)

| Scenario | Likelihood | What it looks like | Signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grinding siege** | Most likely | Bamako supplied only by escorted convoys; the centre keeps bleeding; periodic mass-casualty attacks | Fuel prices, convoy losses, more Dioura-style raids |
| **Quiet local deals** | Possible | Local truces or informal arrangements with JNIM to keep roads open | Sudden drops in attacks on specific routes; prisoner releases |
| **Regime fracture** | Lower, but rising | Army discontent after mass losses leads to a reshuffle or palace coup | Purges, unexplained arrests of officers, shifts in Russian posture |
| **Strategic breakthrough by the state** | Unlikely | Army retakes and holds the rural centre | Would need more troops, less abuse and local political deals, none of which is visible now |

---

## 5. What to watch
1. **The Dioura hostages:** release, exchange or execution. Any of these shapes army morale.
2. **Sévaré airport:** if it's degraded, the army's air bridge to the north weakens.
3. **Fuel prices and convoy losses** into Bamako.
4. **More abuse reports** from Mopti and Ségou, as a recruitment signal for JNIM.
5. **Officer purges or cabinet changes** in Bamako, as a stability signal.
6. **JNIM–FLA cohesion,** especially over Kidal and Gao.

---

## 6. The Africa Blind Spot angle
Mali's army lost more than 100 soldiers in one attack this month. Its defence minister was assassinated at home in April. Its capital is under a fuel blockade by an al-Qaeda affiliate. And in Mali, **saying so on television can get you two years in prison.**

**Suggested line:** *"Mali isn't quiet. It's been silenced."*
