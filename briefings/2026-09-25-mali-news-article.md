# Mali's army suffers one of its worst defeats in years as militants tighten grip on the country's centre

**At least 100 soldiers killed in a single raid; more than 90 believed held captive. Rights groups accuse government and Russian forces of killing civilians in the same regions.**

*Africa Blind Spot · Published 25 September 2026 · Compiled from wire, rights-group and research reporting. Africa Blind Spot did not report from Mali.*

---

An al-Qaeda-linked group killed at least 100 Malian soldiers in an attack on an army camp in central Mali this month, according to security and diplomatic sources. It was one of the deadliest single blows to the country's military in years, and it deepens doubts about the ruling junta's promise to restore security.

The attack on the camp at Dioura, in the Mopti region, took place on 10 September. A security source told Reuters that 130 soldiers died and more than 100 were taken hostage. A diplomatic source put the death toll at around 120. Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM), which claimed the attack, said it killed 150 soldiers, including two colonels, and captured 93. The group released videos of dozens of men it said were captured Malian soldiers.

The Associated Press said it could not verify the figures. A spokesperson for Mali's army declined to comment. Mali's government has not published a death toll.

Five personnel from Russia's Africa Corps, the Kremlin-controlled force that replaced the Wagner Group in Mali, were also killed, according to several reports.

## Pressure on the centre

The Dioura raid is part of a wider escalation across Mali's central regions of Ségou and Mopti. That belt of farmland, river delta and trade routes connects the capital, Bamako, in the south with the desert north.

- **9 August:** JNIM attacked a garrison at San, in Ségou, killing 10 soldiers, according to reports compiled by conflict monitors.
- **September:** JNIM said it had struck air-defence systems at the airport in Sévaré, a key military hub, and killed 33 pro-government fighters around Sévaré and Konna. Those claims could not be independently verified.
- Dioura itself was attacked in 2023 and 2025.

The Africa Center for Strategic Studies, a US Defense Department research institution, says the attacks are part of a "long trajectory of worsening security" rather than isolated events.
- It estimates that annual deaths linked to militant Islamist groups in Mali **have tripled** since the military took power, to about **2,650** in the past year.
- It says the insurgent threat "exceeds the capacity of the Mali military to counter on its own".

## A capital under blockade

The fighting in the centre comes as JNIM keeps up a year-long fuel blockade of southern Mali:
- The blockade began in **September 2025**.
- In **April 2026**, the group declared a full blockade of Bamako.
- More than **300 fuel tankers** have been destroyed, according to open-source tallies.

Mali is landlocked and imports about 95% of its fuel by road from Senegal and Côte d'Ivoire. The army has escorted more than 1,000 tankers into the capital in armed convoys protected by Russian forces and drones. Residents describe fuel queues and rationing, and the capital is noticeably darker at night.

The Africa Center has described the attacks as an explicit economic strategy to isolate Bamako.

The pressure extends to the heart of the state:
- **25 April:** JNIM and the Tuareg separatist Azawad Liberation Front (FLA) launched coordinated attacks on Bamako, the nearby garrison town of Kati, Mopti, Gao and Kidal.
- **Defence Minister General Sadio Camara** was killed when a suicide bomber drove a vehicle into his home in Kati.
- The FLA took control of **Kidal**, the northern city the army had recaptured in 2023.

## The government's position

Mali's transitional president, General Assimi Goïta, marked the country's 66th independence anniversary on 22 September by pledging to intensify the fight against armed groups.
- He cited "proactive" military operations, reforms within the armed forces, new equipment and expanded training.
- He reaffirmed security cooperation with Burkina Faso and Niger through the Alliance of Sahel States.
- He called for "dialogue and reconciliation" among Malians and said Mali would keep defending its "sovereign choices".

Since seizing power in coups in 2020 and 2021, Goïta's government has:
- expelled French forces and ended the United Nations peacekeeping mission,
- turned to Russia for military support,
- ended the 2015 Algiers peace agreement with northern armed groups in January 2024,
- and left the West African bloc ECOWAS.

The government argues that a decade of French and UN-led operations failed to defeat the insurgency, and that Mali must decide its own security policy. Pro-junta rallies in Bamako in recent years have shown that this message has a real following.

## Civilians caught between the forces

Human rights groups say civilians in central Mali are being killed by both sides:
- **15 June:** An Africa Corps airstrike on the village of Kyrnia, in Mopti, killed eight civilians, including three children, according to **Human Rights Watch**.
- **10 July:** Africa Corps fighters, accompanied by Malian soldiers, stormed the village of Bombori, also in Mopti. They rounded up at least 80 men and boys from the ethnic Fulani neighbourhood and killed nine civilians, including four children, according to HRW. One was beheaded.
- **Mid-September:** Local sources cited in media reports said an army unit executed **11 civilians** in Ke-Macina, in Ségou, and threw their bodies into a river. Africa Blind Spot could not independently confirm the report.

Africa Blind Spot found no public response from Malian authorities to these specific allegations.

Violence against civilians in central Mali has a long history:
- In March 2022, **more than 500 people** were killed, most of them summarily executed, during a five-day operation by Malian troops and "foreign military personnel" in the village of **Moura**, according to a **UN human rights** fact-finding report. The government denied UN investigators access to the village.
- Jihadist groups and ethnic militias have also carried out massacres in the region.

Analysts have long warned that abuses against Fulani communities, whom security forces often accuse of sympathising with JNIM's local unit, Katiba Macina, help the group recruit.

## Humanitarian toll

- About **415,000** people were internally displaced in Mali as of April 2026, according to aid agencies. They are concentrated in Gao, Ménaka, Mopti, Bandiagara, Timbuktu and San.
- A June 2026 food security assessment found that **40% of households** needed humanitarian assistance, but only **4%** had received any in the previous three months.
- The UN's humanitarian appeal for Mali was about **7% funded** as of late June.

## Reporting under pressure

Independent reporting on the conflict is increasingly restricted:
- **14 September:** A cybercrime court sentenced journalist **Abdramane Keïta**, director of the newspaper *Le Témoin*, to two years in prison. He was arrested in June, days after saying on television that JNIM controlled a northern city. Human Rights Watch called the charges "bogus".
- **August:** A prominent journalist was jailed for **seven years** for criticising the junta.

The Africa Center says official restrictions on reporting mean fatality figures across the Sahel are likely an undercount.

## What comes next

"The alliance between JNIM and FLA poses a threat to the government's consolidation of national territory, which is a key goal of the military," Beverly Ochieng, senior analyst at Control Risks, told Al Jazeera.

The Sahel now accounts for 42% of deaths linked to militant Islamist groups across Africa, according to the Africa Center. JNIM is spreading south toward coastal West African states. In June, the group attacked the airport in Niamey, the capital of neighbouring Niger.

Mali's army has not said what happened to the soldiers shown in JNIM's videos.

### What we don't know
- The exact death toll at Dioura. Figures range from about 100 to 150, and the government has published none.
- The fate of the soldiers JNIM says it captured.
- Whether the Ke-Macina killings happened as described.
- The full extent of Russian losses.
