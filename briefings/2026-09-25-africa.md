# Africa Briefing — Friday, 25 September 2026

*Key events across the continent over the past 24–72 hours. Items dated before today are marked as context.*

---

## Top stories

### 1. Africa presses for permanent UN Security Council seats at UNGA 81
- **Nigeria:** In the General Debate in New York, President Bola Tinubu (in a speech delivered by Vice President Kashim Shettima) called for **at least two permanent Security Council seats for Africa, with veto power**. He said the current structure no longer reflects how global power is distributed.
- **Senegal:** President Bassirou Diomaye Faye spoke as the current ECOWAS chair. He warned that "the Sahel must not be abandoned to its desolate fate," called for a collective international response to cross-border terrorism, pressed for greater African representation and **formally endorsed Macky Sall's candidacy for UN Secretary-General**.
- *Context:* On 22 September, UN Secretary-General António Guterres said Africa's lack of a permanent seat is "no longer defensible."

### 2. U.S.–Nigeria critical minerals framework signed
- Nigeria's Minister of Solid Minerals Development, Dele Alake, and U.S. Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau signed a **framework agreement to attract U.S. investment into Nigeria's mineral resource base, estimated at about $700 billion**. They signed it at Nigeria's Mission House in New York, on the sidelines of UNGA.
- It covers geological data and exploration, processing, infrastructure and technical capacity. Abuja stressed that it wants local processing and value addition, not only raw-material exports.

### 3. DR Congo Ebola epidemic: record toll, mixed trends
- As of 22 September, the DRC had reported **7,773 confirmed cases and 3,759 deaths**. This makes it the largest and fastest-spreading Ebola outbreak on record, surpassing the 2014 West Africa epidemic.
- **Ituri** is the epicentre, with 5,966 cases and 2,737 deaths in 28 of its 36 health zones. Cases there **fell about 26% over the last 21 days**, but **North Kivu rose about 73%**, and the virus has spread into Haut-Uele.
- The strain is *Bundibugyo ebolavirus*. Existing vaccines and treatments target the Zaire strain, which makes the response harder. This week the U.S. State Department pledged **$267 million**.
- **Kenya:** Today the U.S. Embassy in Nairobi warned of a **possible data breach** affecting Ebola screening records collected at Kenyan ports of entry. The data may include names, passport numbers and health information. Travellers who arrived from 10 regional countries since late May could be affected.

### 4. Ethiopia: new rebel alliance and continuing drone strikes
- On 20 September, seven armed groups formed the **Ethiopian People's Forces Alliance for Survival (EPFAS)**. Members include the TPLF, the Amhara Fano National Movement, the OLA and the ONLF, plus Afar, Benishangul and Gumuz movements.
- Government drone strikes continued. On 22 September, strikes reportedly hit two schools in Alamata, **killing or injuring more than 30 civilians**. An earlier strike destroyed a grain-aid convoy in northwestern Tigray.
- The UN says it is "deeply concerned" about the escalation.

## By region

### West Africa
- **Nigeria:** At the All Nigeria Editors Conference in Enugu, speakers warned that courts deciding election outcomes, weak institutions and AI-generated disinformation threaten the credibility of the **2027 elections**. Tinubu also backed the proposed **Gateway Deep Seaport and Ogun Blue Marine Special Economic Zone**.
- **Niger (context):** On 17 September, junta leader Abdourahamane Tiani signed a decree **revoking the nationality of prominent Nigeriens living abroad**.

### East Africa
- **Uganda / South Sudan:** Uganda has started implementing the **South Sudan–Uganda Power Interconnection Project (SUPIP)**, backed by **US$121 million from the African Development Fund**. The project will support cross-border power trade and strengthen northern Uganda's grid.
- **Kenya:** The national team opens its **AFCON 2027 qualifying campaign against Eritrea on Saturday, 26 September**.

### Central Africa
- **Central African Republic (context, 23 Sept):** The ICC convicted former Séléka commander **Mahamat Said Abdel Kani** of crimes against humanity, including torture, persecution and unlawful imprisonment, for abuses at the OCRB in Bangui in 2013. He was acquitted of three war-crimes charges. A sentencing hearing is set for November, and he faces a possible life sentence.

### Southern Africa
- **South Africa:** In his Heritage Day speech on 24 September, President Cyril Ramaphosa promised to **address the nation within days on gender-based violence**. The speech followed the discovery of **nine women's bodies around Kempton Park, Ekurhuleni**, over nine weeks. "I am ashamed of what we men are doing to the women of South Africa," he said. A specialised multidisciplinary team is now leading the investigation.
- **South Africa:** A suspect linked to the **2021 Sydenham killings of nine people** was due to appear in the Durban Magistrate's Court today.
- **Weather:** The South African Weather Service issued a **Yellow Level 2 warning** for severe thunderstorms and heavy rain over central and western parts of the country, running from Friday to Sunday morning.

### Horn / Sudan
- **Sudan:** The war between the army and the RSF continues. UNHCR published its weekly update on refugee arrivals in neighbouring countries today. RSF drone strikes on cities such as El Obeid, Omdurman and Khartoum have been a recurring pattern since August.
