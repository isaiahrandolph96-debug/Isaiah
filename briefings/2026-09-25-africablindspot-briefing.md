# AFRICA BLIND SPOT: The Briefing
**Friday, 25 September 2026 (evening edition)** · *The stories others miss*

## Lead story: Ethiopia's war is back, and Tigray has gone dark
- **What's new today:** Internet and phone networks went down across Tigray on Thursday night. State-owned Ethio Telecom is fully cut; the smaller private operator, Safaricom, is still working. Reuters sources say the TPLF ordered the cut. A source close to the TPLF denies this and blames the government.
- **The fighting:** The TPLF has declared a "full-blown war" on the federal government. New clashes are reported on the Kobo–Tumuga front, where Tigrayan forces are fighting alongside Amhara Fano militia, their enemies in the last war.
- **How we got here:**
  - **20 Sept:** Seven armed groups formed an alliance to topple PM Abiy Ahmed.
  - **23 Sept:** Tigrayan forces seized the airports at Mekelle, Axum and Shire, and Ethiopian Airlines cancelled all flights to the region.
  - **23 Sept, at the UN:** President Taye Atske Selassie said Ethiopia had "exercised immense restraint".
- **Why it matters:** The 2022 Pretoria deal ended a war that the African Union's mediator says may have killed up to 600,000 people. That war also started with a communications blackout, then famine.
- **Watch:** Whether Eritrea is drawn in. The US let its Eritrea sanctions lapse on 18 Sept (they were imposed over the last Tigray war).

## 2. Super El Niño: Africa's Sahel faces the deadliest heat
- A University of Chicago Climate Impact Lab study projects **about 451,000 extra heat deaths worldwide** between now and February 2027.
- **Nigeria tops the world** at about **31,400** projected deaths.
- The next three are also in the Sahel: **Sudan (17,500), Niger (10,000) and Chad (8,000)**.
- **Blind-spot angle:** The countries least responsible for global heating face the highest toll, and this barely made global headlines.

## 3. DR Congo Ebola: second-largest outbreak on record
- **7,773 confirmed cases and 3,759 deaths**, as of 22 Sept.
- Cases are falling in Ituri (down about 26% in 21 days) but **rising about 73% in North Kivu**.
- There is no approved vaccine or treatment for this strain (Bundibugyo).
- **Kenya:** The US Embassy warned today of a **possible data breach** of Ebola screening records taken at Kenyan ports of entry.

## 4. Africa at the UN: "Give us a permanent seat"
- **Nigeria:** Called for **at least two permanent Security Council seats for Africa, with veto power**.
- **Senegal:** President Faye warned that "the Sahel must not be abandoned to its desolate fate" and endorsed **Macky Sall for UN Secretary-General**.
- **Nigeria and the US:** Signed a critical-minerals framework to draw investment into a resource base Nigeria estimates at about **$700 billion**.

## 5. South Africa: the country waits for Ramaphosa's address on violence against women
- The bodies of **nine women** have been found around Ekurhuleni over about two months.
- On Heritage Day, Ramaphosa called gender-based violence a "national shame" and promised to address the nation **within days**, with more money, resources and technology for the fight.

## Quick hits
- **Morocco:** Provisional election results put **PAM first with 97 of 395 seats**, on a turnout of **38%** (down from 50% in 2021). Coalition talks are next.
- **Madagascar:** One year after Gen Z protests brought down President Rajoelina, activists say they feel **"betrayed"** by the military-backed government that replaced him. Several were arrested earlier this year.
- **Uganda / South Sudan:** Work has started on a **$121 million** power line (African Development Fund) linking the two grids.
- **Central African Republic:** The ICC convicted ex-Séléka commander **Mahamat Said Abdel Kani** of crimes against humanity. Sentencing is due in November.
- **Kenya:** The national team plays **Eritrea on Saturday** in AFCON 2027 qualifying.
