# Ethiopia: The War That Came Back (in depth)
**Africa Blind Spot · as of Friday 25 September 2026, evening**

> **In one paragraph:** Four years after a peace deal ended one of the deadliest wars of the century, northern Ethiopia is at war again. Tigray's ruling party (the TPLF) has joined six other armed groups, including its old enemy, Amhara's Fano militia, in an alliance to overthrow Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed. On 23 September, Tigrayan forces seized three airports, and fighting spread into Afar and Amhara. By Friday, Tigray's phone and internet networks were down, roads to Afar were closed and people were fleeing. Meanwhile, Abiy's push for a port on Eritrea's coast threatens to turn a civil war into a regional one.

---

## 1. The timeline

| Date | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Nov 2020** | Abiy sends the army into Tigray after months of rising tension. Eritrean troops and Amhara forces fight on the federal side. |
| **2020–2022** | Tigray is sealed off: no phones, banking or internet, and little food or medicine. Famine spreads. The AU's mediator, Olusegun Obasanjo, later estimates **up to 600,000 deaths**. |
| **2 Nov 2022** | The **Pretoria agreement** (AU-brokered) ends the war. Eritrea is not a party to it. |
| **2023** | Fano, which fought for the government, turns against it in Amhara. |
| **2024–2025** | The TPLF splits internally. In **April 2025**, Lt. Gen. **Tadesse Werede** replaces **Getachew Reda** as head of the Tigray Interim Administration. |
| **Jan–Feb 2026** | Clashes in Tigray. |
| **Apr 2026** | The federal government extends the interim administration's mandate by a year. The TPLF says this violates Pretoria. |
| **May 2026** | The TPLF reinstates Tigray's regional council, which elects TPLF chair **Debretsion Gebremichael** as president. Tigray now has **two rival leaderships**. |
| **1 Jun 2026** | National election. Abiy's Prosperity Party wins a landslide (438 seats). **Tigray does not vote.** |
| **1 Aug 2026** | Hours-long battle between the army and the TPLF near the Sudan border. |
| **Early Sept 2026** | Abiy says it is only "a matter of time" before Ethiopia regains access to Eritrea's port of **Assab**. |
| **18–19 Sept** | The US lets its 2021 Eritrea sanctions (imposed over the Tigray war) lapse. |
| **20 Sept** | **Seven armed groups form the Ethiopian People's Forces Alliance for Survival (EPFAS)** to overthrow Abiy and install a transitional government. |
| **22 Sept** | Government drone and air strikes. Two strikes hit schools in **Alamata**: **at least 14 dead and 22 injured**, according to local authorities. |
| **23 Sept** | Tigrayan forces seize the **Mekelle, Axum and Shire airports**. Ethiopian Airlines cancels all Tigray flights. Offensives hit Afar and Amhara (Zata, Dinqa, Soqota and Tonsa). |
| **23 Sept, New York** | President **Taye Atske Selassie** tells the UN that Ethiopia has "exercised immense restraint" despite "interference". |
| **24–25 Sept** | Tigray's **phone and internet networks go down**. Roads from Tigray to Afar close to civilians. Fighting on the **Kobo–Tumuga front**. The TPLF declares a "full-blown war". |

## 2. Who's fighting whom

### The government side
- **PM Abiy Ahmed** and the **Ethiopian National Defense Force (ENDF)**. He was freshly re-elected in June and won the 2019 Nobel Peace Prize for making peace with Eritrea.
- The federal government uses **drones and airstrikes** heavily.

### The rebel alliance (EPFAS), formed 20 September
1. **TPLF / Tigray Defense Forces**: Tigray's ruling party. It ran Ethiopia for about 30 years until 2018.
2. **Amhara Fano National Movement**: fought *for* the government in 2020–22, then against it since 2023.
3. **Oromo Liberation Army (OLA)**: Oromia region.
4. **Ogaden National Liberation Front (ONLF)**: Somali region.
5. **An Afar group**: named differently by different outlets.
6. **Benishangul People's Liberation Movement**.
7. **Gumuz People's Democratic Movement**.

**Why it matters:** This alliance spans Ethiopia's north, west, south and east. TPLF spokesman **Michael Asgedom** told AP the groups will cooperate "politically and militarily", and that Pretoria is "no longer valid".

**Why it may not hold (per CNN and experts):** The TPLF and Fano are still divided over **Western Tigray**. Amhara forces took this fertile border land in the last war, and both sides claim it. The members also want different things.

### Inside Tigray: a divided house
- **Tadesse Werede** heads the federally recognised interim administration and is seen as more cautious.
- **Debretsion Gebremichael** chairs the TPLF and was elected regional president by the reinstated council.
- Analysts describe a **hardline faction** pushing for confrontation and a **cautious faction** backed by Tadesse. The hardliners appear to have won the argument.

---

## 3. Who started it?
Both sides tell a different story:
- **The TPLF** says government forces fired first, and calls its war "defensive".
- **Addis Ababa** says the rebels ordered an "all-out offensive" on federal positions in Afar and Amhara.
- **Independent view:** Human Rights Watch says key parts of the Pretoria deal were violated for years. It points in particular to the **continued forced expulsion of ethnic Tigrayans from Western Tigray**.

*Africa Blind Spot has no independent confirmation of who fired first. Report it as disputed.*

---

## 4. The human cost, before and now
- **Still displaced from the last war:** more than **500,000 people** in Tigray, dependent on aid. In March 2026, regional officials said **over 1,300 displaced people had died of hunger and lack of medicine** in the camps as aid shrank. In June, displaced people held a three-day protest in Mekelle demanding to go home.
- **This week:**
  - The Alamata school strikes (at least 14 dead).
  - People fleeing Tigray into Afar. "Too many people are being displaced," says Valerie Browning of the Afar Pastoralist Development Association.
  - Market prices rising in Mekelle. A garlic seller told AP: "There is an atmosphere of fear."
- **The blackout:**
  - State-owned Ethio Telecom is completely cut; the smaller private operator, Safaricom, is still up.
  - Reuters sources say the TPLF ordered the cut. A source close to the TPLF denies it and blames the government.
  - Either way, it echoes 2020–22, when a blackout hid famine from the world.

---

## 5. The regional danger: Eritrea, the Red Sea and beyond
- **Assab:** Ethiopia has been landlocked since Eritrea's independence in 1993. Abiy calls losing the port a "mistake" and says regaining access is "a matter of time". Eritrea sees this as a threat to its sovereignty.
- **Old allies turned rivals:** Eritrea fought *with* Ethiopia in 2020–22 but was left out of Pretoria. Relations have collapsed since. Ethiopia accuses Eritrea of backing the rebels; Eritrea denies it.
- **Washington's shift:** The US let its Eritrea sanctions lapse on 18 September "to advance US regional interests". Eritrea has more than 1,000 km of Red Sea coast facing Yemen.
- **Possible camps (analysts' assessment, not fact):** Critical Threats suggests Egypt, Eritrea, Saudi Arabia, Somalia's federal government and Sudan's army could lean toward the rebels. The UAE and Sudan's RSF could lean toward Addis Ababa. Egypt and Ethiopia are also at odds over the **Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam** on the Nile.
- **Next door:** Sudan's own war is in its fourth year. A second war in the Horn would put two of Africa's most populous countries in conflict at once.

---

## 6. How the world is responding
- **African Union:** called for an immediate end to the fighting.
- **UN and EU:** called for restraint and a return to the AU-led peace process.
- **United States:** warned that more leaders could face **sanctions** if they escalate.
- **United Kingdom:** tightened its travel advice from 24 September.
- **Ethiopia's government:** blames "reckless external actors" and "interference".

---

## 7. What to watch next
1. **The blackout:** does it spread or last? It's a key warning sign for famine and hidden atrocities.
2. **Eritrea:** any troop movements or border incidents.
3. **Fighting spreading** into Amhara, Afar and Oromia, where other alliance members are active.
4. **Western Tigray:** the likeliest place for the alliance to crack.
5. **Mediation:** can the AU, possibly with the US, restart talks?
6. **Aid access:** whether humanitarian convoys and fuel reach Tigray. An earlier strike destroyed a grain-aid convoy in north-western Tigray.

---

## 8. Why this is a blind spot
- The **last** Tigray war may have killed up to 600,000 people, more than many conflicts that dominated global headlines, yet it got a fraction of the coverage.
- It happened largely **behind a communications blackout**. This week, the lines went down again.
- This week, Ethiopia's war shared the news cycle with the UN General Assembly, El Niño and Ebola.

**The Africa Blind Spot line:** *"Last time, the world noticed too late. This time, the warning signs are loud."*
