# Field Report: single-story brief, 30 September 2026

## Nigeria: taken from the groundnut harvest, and a baby born in captivity

*Mariga, Niger State, and Gusau, Zamfara State: farming country in northern Nigeria*

It was harvest time in Tungan Rimi, a farming village in Niger State, in Nigeria's north-central region. Women were out in the fields pulling up groundnuts (peanuts) when armed men arrived. "Bandits invaded the area and assembled them and took them away," the local government chairman, Aliyu Bagga Muhammadu, told Daily Trust. The gunmen then spotted men working on nearby farms and took them too.

Now at least one of those women is a mother. She gave birth in the kidnappers' camp, Muhammadu told Punch and Channels Television. Her family has not heard from her. Relatives say they have had no direct contact with anyone who was taken.

More than 80 people, most of them women, were kidnapped in two attacks in northern Nigeria over the weekend, the news agency AFP reported on Monday, citing security reports and a local source. In Niger State, at least 47 farmers were taken from Tungan Rimi, in the Mariga area. The next day, about 40 women were taken from Yargada, a village near Gusau, the capital of Zamfara State, according to a security report prepared for the United Nations and seen by AFP.

The numbers are disputed. The Mariga chairman says 47 were taken, and Punch reports "more than 47". The Niger State police say "over twenty persons" were abducted at about 3pm on 27 September at Mukura village, in the same district. In Zamfara, the police spokesman, Yazid Abubakar, told the Nigerian newspaper Leadership that the true number "might be less than what members of the communities claimed". Residents told AFP it was likely higher.

### Four hours, and no help

In Yargada, the gunmen stayed for more than four hours, according to a security source quoted by Rocketparrot News. They killed one resident, burned six vehicles and broke into shops. Residents called the authorities while the attack was going on. They say no security forces came.

The raids spread to neighbouring villages that night. In Magama Mairake, gunmen arrived around midnight, fired shots and searched house to house, Leadership reported. A survivor, Lawali Kutsa Ada, said about 44 people were taken, mostly women and children, and about 10 vehicles were set on fire. "The bandits have been burning homes in Magama Mairake since yesterday up till this moment," another resident, Adamu Lawwali, told AFP.

Two residents told AFP the attack was revenge, because local vigilantes (volunteer village guards) had recently killed several bandits.

### A business built on people

The attackers are what Nigerians call "bandits": armed gangs on motorcycles who kidnap people for ransom and steal cattle. For years they have raided villages across Nigeria's northwest and north-central regions, looting, burning homes and taking captives into the forests.

The gangs have already named their price. They are asking for 2 million naira for each farmer, about $1,500, Muhammadu said. For 47 people, that is almost 100 million naira. In a farming village, that is a sum few families could ever raise.

Kidnapping pays. SBM Intelligence, a research company based in Lagos, found that armed groups collected about 7.8 billion naira (nearly $6 million) in ransoms between July 2025 and June 2026. That is more than three times the previous year's total, AFP reported.

This weekend was not a one-off. In August, gunmen took as many as 600 worshippers during Friday prayers at a mosque in Dekera, also in Niger State. Last week, families there protested after the state governor said only a few dozen had been taken. Locals say the real number is nearly 550, AFP reported. Many have been held for more than a month.

### Why the world should be watching

Nigeria is Africa's most populous country, and it votes for a president in January. Security is expected to be one of the biggest election issues, along with the rising cost of living, and President Bola Tinubu faces growing criticism for failing to protect villages. The government points to successes against armed groups, but researchers say the violence is getting worse, AFP reported.

There is also a hidden cost. Harvest season is when farmers have to go out into remote fields. When the fields are where people get taken, food itself becomes a risk.

### What happens next

Soldiers and police have gone into the forest to search, the Niger State police spokesman, Wasiu Abiodun, told Punch on Wednesday. Joint teams and local vigilantes have already fought the gunmen once, near Alibani village, and a vigilante was wounded. Witnesses say the kidnappers moved into the Bariki forest, also known as Ma'undu, where the captives are thought to be held.

In Zamfara, police say a search and rescue team has been sent out. Somewhere in those forests is a woman who went out to harvest groundnuts and is now caring for a newborn in a kidnappers' camp.
