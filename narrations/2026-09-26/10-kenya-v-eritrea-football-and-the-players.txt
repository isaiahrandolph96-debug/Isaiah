Today in Nairobi, Kenya plays Eritrea in football. But for Eritrea, just getting onto the pitch is a story.

Kenya's national team, the Harambee Stars, hosts Eritrea at Nyayo Stadium in a qualifier for the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations. Kenya is co-hosting that tournament, with Uganda and Tanzania, so it has already qualified. These matches are its preparation.

For Eritrea, a small country on the Red Sea coast of the Horn of Africa, this match means much more. In March, Eritrea played its first international match in six years, and beat Eswatini to reach this stage.

But after that win, only three of the ten locally based players returned home. The other seven reportedly disappeared in South Africa on the way back.

It is not the first time. The United Nations estimates that around 80 Eritrean footballers, coaches and officials have defected during tournaments over two decades. They cite political repression, and military service with no end date.

So when Eritrea's players walk out today, many people will be watching the scoreline, and the team sheet on the way home.
