Sudan's army chief was expected to address the world at the United Nations. Instead, he is stuck at home.

Sudan, in north-east Africa, has been at war since April 2023. On one side is the national army, led by General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan. On the other side is a powerful paramilitary group called the Rapid Support Forces, or RSF. The war has forced millions of people from their homes.

This week, the United States did not issue General Burhan a visa to attend the UN General Assembly in New York.

According to a Sudanese diplomatic source and a Western analyst, the visa was tied to a condition: that Burhan sign a 90-day ceasefire proposed by the American envoy Massad Boulos. Burhan reportedly refused.

Sudan says barring a head of state breaks the agreement that hosts the UN in New York. UN Secretary-General António Guterres has expressed regret and called for the war to end.

Meanwhile, the fighting goes on. In the city of El Obeid, in the Kordofan region, around half a million people have been trapped as the RSF tries to take control of the surrounding roads.

The question now is whether pressure from Washington brings a ceasefire closer, or pushes it further away.
