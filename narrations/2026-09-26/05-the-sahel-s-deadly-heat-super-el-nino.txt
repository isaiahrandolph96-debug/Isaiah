The deadliest weather event of the coming months may not be a storm. It may be heat.

Every few years, the Pacific Ocean warms in a pattern called El Niño, which shifts weather around the world. This year's is expected to be unusually strong, a so-called super El Niño.

A new study from the University of Chicago's Climate Impact Lab estimates it could cause around 451,000 extra heat-related deaths worldwide between now and February 2027.

And the country projected to suffer the most is Nigeria, with about 31,400 deaths.

The next three are also in Africa's Sahel, the dry belt south of the Sahara. Sudan, with about 17,500. Niger, about 10,000. And Chad, about 8,000.

These are estimates, not certainties. But they point to a hard truth. The countries that have done the least to heat the planet are among those expected to pay the highest price.

Researchers say much of this is preventable. Early heat warnings, access to water and shade, cooling centres, and protecting people who work outdoors can save lives.

The question is whether that help arrives before the heat does.
