YASMINE'S VOTE
A short film for Africa Blind Spot
Runtime: about 90 seconds · Vertical 9:16 · Dramatization based on real events
Facts: provisional results of Morocco's 23 September 2026 election

On-screen note at the start: "Dramatization. Yasmine and her grandfather are fictional. The events and figures are real."


FADE IN:

EXT. ROOFTOP, CASABLANCA - DAWN

Laundry lines, satellite dishes, the call to prayer fading over the city. YASMINE (24) sits on the ledge in a hoodie, turning a voter card over in her fingers.

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          This is Yasmine. She's twenty-four,
          she has an engineering degree, and
          she has never had a job.

Yasmine looks toward the horizon. In the distance, cranes stand over a half-built football stadium.

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          Today, Morocco is choosing a new
          parliament. And Yasmine hasn't
          decided if she'll vote.

ON SCREEN: 23 SEPTEMBER 2026 · ELECTION DAY


EXT. BOULEVARD, CASABLANCA - NIGHT - ONE YEAR EARLIER

A crowd of young people marches, phone lights raised. Yasmine is among them, holding a cardboard sign: "HOSPITALS, NOT STADIUMS."

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          A year ago, she was in the streets.
          Thousands of young Moroccans marched
          under the name "Gen Z 212", for jobs,
          for hospitals, for schools.

                    YASMINE
               (shouting with the crowd)
          We don't want stadiums! We want
          hospitals!

ON SCREEN: SEPT-OCT 2025 · GEN Z 212 PROTESTS


INT. FAMILY APARTMENT - MORNING

Mint tea steams on a low table. Yasmine's grandfather, HAJ AHMED (74), buttons his best djellaba. He picks up his voter card like it's a plane ticket.

                    HAJ AHMED
          Get your shoes on. We're voting.

                    YASMINE
          Jiddo, you vote every time. What
          changes?

                    HAJ AHMED
          Every time, I get a say. The King
          chooses the head of government, but
          he chooses from the party that wins
          the most seats. So the seats matter.

                    YASMINE
          The seats went to the same people
          last time.

He pauses at the door.

                    HAJ AHMED
          Then change the seats.


EXT. SCHOOL COURTYARD / POLLING STATION - DAY

A classroom turned polling station. Rows of voting booths, most of them empty. Haj Ahmed walks in proudly. Yasmine stops at the gate, hands in her pockets.

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          Haj Ahmed voted. Yasmine walked home.
          She wasn't alone.

ON SCREEN: TURNOUT 38% (2021: 50%)

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          More than six in ten registered
          voters stayed home.


INT. FAMILY APARTMENT - NIGHT - NEXT DAY

The TV glows. On the news, the Interior Minister reads the results. Haj Ahmed leans forward; Yasmine watches from the doorway, pretending not to care.

ON SCREEN (as the numbers are read):
PAM 97 · RNI 66 · ISTIQLAL 65 · PJD 54

                    HAJ AHMED
          PAM first! Ninety-seven seats.

                    YASMINE
          And the R-N-I, Akhannouch's party?
          Sixty-six. They had over a hundred.

                    HAJ AHMED
          People remembered your march.

The TV cuts to cheering supporters of the Islamist PJD.

                    HAJ AHMED (CONT'D)
               (laughing, shaking his head)
          And Benkirane is back! Thirteen seats
          last time, fifty-four now.

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          The Islamist PJD quadrupled its seats,
          and says it will stay in opposition.


INT. FAMILY APARTMENT - LATER

Haj Ahmed pushes the tea glasses aside and lines up sugar cubes on the tray like a parliament.

                    HAJ AHMED
          Look. There are three hundred and
          ninety-five seats. To govern, you
          need one hundred and ninety-eight.
          PAM has ninety-seven. Not enough.

He slides more cubes in, one pile at a time.

                    HAJ AHMED (CONT'D)
          So they need partners. Istiqlal...
          the Popular Movement... maybe the
          Constitutional Union.

                    YASMINE
          Why not the R-N-I again?

                    HAJ AHMED
          PAM says no.

Yasmine sits down across from him, finally interested.

ON SCREEN: NO COALITION AGREED YET


INT. FAMILY APARTMENT - CONTINUOUS

On the TV: PAM leader FATIMA-ZAHRA EL MANSOURI speaks at a podium (news footage).

                    YASMINE
               (quietly)
          If the King chooses her, she'd be
          the first woman to run our government.

                    HAJ AHMED
          And only the second in the whole Arab
          world. After Tunisia.

Yasmine studies the screen.


EXT. ROOFTOP, CASABLANCA - NIGHT

Yasmine is back on the ledge. The city glitters. The stadium site blazes with floodlights; beside it, a hospital sits dim.

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          Morocco has changed who leads its
          parliament. But the questions from the
          streets haven't changed. Where are the
          jobs? When do hospitals and schools
          get better?

Yasmine takes out her voter card, looks at it, and puts it in her pocket instead of throwing it away.

                    YASMINE
               (to herself)
          Next time, I'm watching what they do
          first.

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          A country of more than thirty-six
          million just reshaped its parliament,
          and it barely made the news. That's
          the blind spot.

                    NARRATOR (V.O.)
          Send this to someone who hasn't heard.

SMASH CUT TO:

BLACK. Gold text:

AFRICA BLIND SPOT
FOLLOW FOR THE STORIES OTHERS MISS

FADE OUT.

FACTS USED IN THE STORY (all real; for your on-screen checks)
- Election day: 23 September 2026. Provisional results announced 24 September by the Interior Minister.
- Parliament: 395 seats. 198 needed to govern.
- PAM 97 (up from 87) · RNI 66 (down from 102) · Istiqlal 65 (down from 81) · PJD 54 (up from 13).
- Turnout 38% (2021: 50%), so more than 6 in 10 registered voters stayed home.
- The King appoints the head of government from the party with the most seats.
- PAM has ruled out another coalition with the RNI. No coalition agreed yet.
- Gen Z 212 protests: late September to mid-October 2025, over jobs, health and education, and anger at World Cup stadium spending.
- If appointed, Fatima-Zahra El Mansouri would be Morocco's first woman head of government and the second in the Arab world (after Tunisia's Najla Bouden).
- Morocco's population: about 36.8 million (2024 census).

UPDATE BEFORE POSTING: if the King names a head of government or a coalition is agreed, change the last two apartment scenes.

Sources: Maroc.ma · Hespress · Al Jazeera · The National · Morocco World News
