# Africa Blind Spot: story polish

The owner sends a rough story most days. Return it **beautifully written, accurate and clear to a first-time viewer**, ready to paste into a text-to-video app. Reference result: `stories/2026-10-11-mapungubwe-buried-twice.md`.

## What to deliver
1. **The script only, first.** A title (2–4 words), then the narration. No production notes inside the script.
2. Under it, **two or three lines at most**: what facts you corrected and any claim to double-check. Nothing else unless asked.
3. Save it to `stories/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md` (script, then a short "Facts corrected" and "Sources" list), commit and push.

## How to write it
- **Hook: 5–7 words, about 3 seconds.** The stake or the twist, no date, no "In 19xx". Best hooks come from the owner's own strongest line (e.g. "And then they buried it again" became "This gold was buried twice. Once by a kingdom. Once by a lie.").
- **Keep the owner's voice and best lines.** Improve, don't replace. Their closing lines usually stay.
- **Short lines for voiceover.** One idea per line, a line break where the narrator breathes. Fragments are fine ("A golden bowl. A golden sceptre.").
- **Real imagery only.** Sensory detail must be true and sourced (landscape, objects, materials, weather on record). Never invent quotes, names, feelings or scenes.
- **Explain everything the first time.** Every place ("Limpopo, South Africa's far north"), group, acronym and system ("apartheid, the system that divided South Africans by race").
- **Build a payoff.** Plant something in the hook that returns later (the "second burial"). End on the meaning, then a short, quotable last line.
- **Length:** 300–450 words (about 2–3 minutes) unless the owner asks for a short cut.
- **Africa Blind Spot framing:** make clear what the world missed or was told wrong.

## Facts first (non-negotiable)
- Check every date, number and superlative. Fix overstatements quietly in the script and list them in the note (e.g. "Dark Ages" for the 1200s, "700 years before colonists" when it was 400).
- Use the source's exact strength for superlatives ("largest kingdom of its time", per UNESCO).
- If you could not verify something, cut it or flag it; never keep it just because it sounds good.
- Prefer live checks with web search; if you wrote from memory, say so in the note.
