0.0THE IDEA

The work nobody films

Everyone posts the start and the finish: the launch, the new job, the finish-line photo. Nobody films the half mile, the long middle stretch where you just keep clocking in. Workwear was made for exactly that stretch, and Halfmile takes its toughness and wears it on the street.

The look comes from your references: black duck canvas worn black-on-black with boots, a tan work jacket over a white tee and light denim, olive cargos, brass zippers, and a beanie pulled low. Halfmile keeps that look tonal and heavy, and adds one brass detail, the split line.

Nobody films the half mile.
Who it's for
  • People who work with their hands or keep long hours: trades, warehouse, night shifts, kitchens
  • Builders in the middle years: first businesses, side hustles, creators with small followings
  • People who dress black-on-black or build outfits around one good jacket
  • Age 18 to 34, US first
Three promises
  • Built heavy. 12 oz cotton duck, triple-stitched seams and brass hardware. It looks better the more you wear it.
  • Tonal first. Black on black, tan on tan. One brass detail and nothing louder.
  • The long middle. Every photo shows someone mid-shift, never posing at the finish line.
0.1NAME & VOICE

Halfmile

The name is the middle of the journey: past the start, nowhere near the finish, and still going. It fits workwear because the brand is about the hours you put in, not the finish-line moment.

Name check, 26 Sept 2026
  • No clothing brand called Halfmile showed up in web searches.
  • Businesses in logistics, video and games use the name, and halfmile.com is taken.
  • Before spending money: search USPTO for HALFMILE and HALF MILE in Class 25 (clothing), and check the Instagram and TikTok handles.
  • Domains to try: wearhalfmile.com, halfmileworks.com, halfmile.co. I haven't checked if they're free.

The voice

Talk like the person next to you on the shift: few words, dry humour, and specific details like the hour, the day count or the fabric weight. Respect the work without making a show of it.

Never

Hustle-culture slogans, “grind never stops”, fake countdown timers, making fun of rest, or dressing up in workwear as a costume. Real work is the point.

0.2IDENTITY

The look

The wordmark is squared, stencil-like lettering drawn as custom geometry. It isn't a font, so Halfmile owns it outright. A brass split line sits between HALF and MILE, the way a brass zipper splits a jacket. The woven tab is the signature: a wide stitched label sewn on the chest pocket of every jacket and on the beanie cuff. The H|M monogram is for small spots, like a tee chest or the inside of a collar.

Keep clear of Carhartt

Your references are mostly Carhartt, so Halfmile has to look like its own brand next to them. Don't use a square chest label or a C-shaped emblem, and don't borrow Carhartt's product names. The Halfmile tab is wide and rectangular, and the brand's signal is the brass split line.

0.4THE MONEY

What it costs

Why the jackets can't be print-on-demand: print-on-demand services don't carry duck canvas work jackets. You can buy blank canvas jackets wholesale from workwear makers like Berne and DRI DUCK, but they come with the maker's own branding and cost $65 to $89 each, which leaves no room for your margin. So the jackets are made to your design by a small-batch factory.

Recommendation

Make the jackets in small batches and sell them on pre-order, and put the basics on print-on-demand. Get quotes from three low-minimum factories (some take 20 to 50 pieces per design), and pay for a sample before you pay for a batch. Open pre-orders once the sample is right, then place the batch order.

Start with one batch: 40 Shift Jackets in black and duck tan. Offer the Night Shift Jacket and the faded black colour as pre-order only, and make them only if at least 20 people order.

0.5LAUNCH

The first 90 days

  1. Weeks 1–228 Sep – 11 Oct

    Name, factories, samples

    Run the USPTO and handle checks. Send these drawings and specs to three low-minimum factories and ask each one for a sample price, a per-jacket price at 40 and 100 units, and lead times. Order the print-on-demand samples and the woven tabs.

  2. Weeks 3–512 Oct – 1 Nov

    Wear-test the samples

    Samples usually take 2 to 4 weeks. Wear each jacket for a week of real work, and wash the basics five times. Fix the fit, then shoot every piece on real people mid-shift: early mornings, loading docks, the walk home.

  3. Week 62 – 8 Nov

    Doors open

    The basics go on sale and jacket pre-orders open, with 24 hours of early access for the waitlist. Every jacket gets a lot number (001 to 040), and the product page shows the ship-by date.

  4. Weeks 7–129 Nov – 20 Dec

    Make the batch

    Place the batch order when pre-orders reach about 25. Bulk production takes 4 to 8 weeks. Post the process as it happens (cutting, stitching, the brass going on) under the banner “Nobody films the half mile”. Push the tee and beanie as holiday gifts.

  5. Week 13+late Dec – Jan

    Ship, then Drop 02

    Ship the numbered jackets and ask every buyer for a photo. Drop 02 is duck canvas carpenter pants and cargos in Moss and black, built around what sold.

Goals to aim for
  • 300 waitlist sign-ups before doors open
  • 25 jacket pre-orders in the first two weeks, which triggers the batch
  • Under 3% of orders with a return or quality complaint
  • The sell-through of each colour, which decides batch two
Legal and pre-order basics
  • Register an LLC, get an EIN and turn on sales tax in Shopify.
  • File the trademark once the search is clear.
  • Show a clear ship-by date on every pre-order. The FTC's mail-order rule requires you to ship when you say you will, or to tell buyers about a delay and let them cancel.
  • Your own jackets need sewn-in labels showing the fibre content, the country of origin, care instructions and your brand name or an FTC registration number (RN).
