0.0THE IDEA

Sell the middle

Gym brands sell the finish line: the personal best, the physique. Luxury basics sell the look of having already arrived. Nobody is selling the part in between, and that is where most people actually are.

Halfmile is for people in the middle of something: month eight of a business, week six of a training block, the second year of an apprenticeship. The clothes are quiet on the outside and heavyweight, made to be worn hard and washed often. The story is one people recognise from their own life.

Nobody films the half mile.
Who it's for
  • Runners and gym regulars in the middle of a training block
  • First-time founders and creators building with a small audience
  • Students, apprentices and tradespeople in the long middle years
  • Age 18 to 34, US first. They buy fewer, better pieces
Three promises
  • Built for repeat. Heavyweight cotton that softens with washing instead of wearing out.
  • Quiet outside, loud inside. Small marks on the front and the message on the back or inside the neck.
  • The middle matters. Every campaign shows people mid-effort, never on the podium.
0.1NAME & VOICE

Halfmile

One word, said the way you'd say it out loud. A half mile is 800 metres, or two laps of a track: long enough to hurt and short enough that you can't pace your way out of it. The name says the brand's idea without needing a tagline.

Name check, 26 Sept 2026
  • No clothing brand called Halfmile showed up in web searches.
  • The name is used by businesses in logistics, video production and games, and halfmile.com is taken.
  • Before spending money: search USPTO for HALFMILE and HALF MILE in Class 25 (clothing) and check the Instagram and TikTok handles.
  • Domains to try: wearhalfmile.com, halfmile.co, halfmileclub.com. I haven't checked if they're free.

The voice

Talk like a training partner: short sentences, real numbers (splits, days, distances), and credit for the effort, not the result. Plain words, said calmly.

Never

Hype words like “beast mode” or “grind never stops”, fake countdown timers, making fun of rest, strings of emoji. The brand is the calm voice at mile 13.

Recommendation

Start with print-on-demand through Printful. Printful prints everything in its own facilities, so quality is more consistent than on Printify's network of independent printers. It also stocks the heavyweight blanks the brand promises. You risk nothing on stock while you find out which designs sell.

Move a design to screen printing once it sells about 100 units per run. Factory data puts the break-even between screen printing and print-on-demand (DTG) at around 80 to 120 units. Only after two small batches sell out should you invest in a custom fit and your own labels.

0.5LAUNCH

The first 90 days

  1. Weeks 1–228 Sep – 11 Oct

    Lock the name

    Run the USPTO search, buy the domain and claim the handles. Set up the business and open Shopify and Printful. Order one sample of every piece.

  2. Weeks 3–412 – 25 Oct

    Wear-test and shoot

    Wash every sample five times and look for fading, shrinking and cracked prints. Photograph the pieces on real people in the middle of something: mid-run, mid-shift, mid-build. Launch a waitlist page that says “Get Drop 01 first”.

  3. Weeks 5–626 Oct – 8 Nov

    Nobody films the half mile

    Post ten short videos of people at the middle of something: mile 13 of a marathon, month 8 of a business. Ask followers to post their own with #MyHalfMile. Give free tees to the leaders of two or three local run clubs.

  4. Week 79 – 15 Nov

    Drop 01 opens

    Open a 10-day sales window, with 24 hours of early access for the waitlist. With print-on-demand you could sell forever, so the scarcity is honest and based on time: the window closes and the designs change.

  5. Weeks 8–1316 Nov – 27 Dec

    Holidays, then Drop 02

    Push the cap and beanie as gifts. Email every buyer and ask for a photo. Plan Drop 02 around what sold, not what you like most.

Goals to aim for
  • 300 waitlist sign-ups before the drop opens
  • 10% of the waitlist buying in the first week
  • Under 3% of orders with a return or quality complaint
  • Sales per design, which decides what moves to screen printing
Legal basics
  • Register an LLC, get an EIN and turn on sales tax in Shopify.
  • File the trademark once the search comes back clear.
  • Print-on-demand blanks keep the manufacturer's label, which lists fibre, origin and care. If you replace it later, your label must carry the same information to meet the FTC's textile and care-label rules.
