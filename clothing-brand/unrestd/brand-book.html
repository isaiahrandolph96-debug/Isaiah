01THE IDEA

The E is missing

UNRESTD is UNRESTED with the E taken out, because we didn't stop for it. The logo is a graffiti tag, thrown up fast like it was done on the way to a shift. Its brass underline ends in a drip, because the paint is still wet: we're not done yet.

The look comes from your references: black duck canvas worn black-on-black with boots, a tan work jacket over a white tee and light denim, olive cargos, brass zippers, and a beanie pulled low. UNRESTD keeps that look tonal and heavy, with one brass detail.

While you slept, we clocked in.
Who it's for
  • Night shifts and early starts: trades, warehouses, kitchens, hospitals, drivers
  • People building something on the side after the day job
  • People who dress black-on-black or build outfits around one good jacket
  • Age 18 to 34, US first
Three promises
  • Built heavy. 12 oz cotton duck, triple-stitched seams and brass hardware. It looks better the more you wear it.
  • Tonal first. Black on black, tan on tan. One brass detail and nothing louder.
  • Restless, not reckless. UNRESTD is about never settling, not about never sleeping.

The voice

Talk like the person next to you on the late shift: few words, dry humour, and specific details like the hour, the day count or the fabric weight. Confident, never loud.

Never

Glamorising burnout, making fun of sleep or rest, “grind never stops” slogans, or fake countdown timers. The message is “not done yet”, not “never stop”.

03IDENTITY

The logo

The logo is a graffiti tag, hand-thrown with a lean, a brass swoosh underneath, and one brass drip to show the paint is still wet. It's built as vector outlines from an open-licence graffiti face (SIL Open Font License, which allows logos), with its own spacing, lift and swoosh. It prints and embroiders without needing the font. It comes in four versions: flat in bone or black, 3D with a brass block shadow for big back prints, and a short UD tag for tee chests and collars. The woven tab carries the tag on every jacket chest and on the beanie cuff.

Using the tag
  • Clear space: leave at least the height of the U empty on every side.
  • Smallest size: 1¼ in (32 mm) wide in print and 1½ in (38 mm) in embroidery. Below that, use the UD tag.
  • On busy photos or colours, use the 3D version. Its outline keeps it readable.
  • The drip is always brass, or the same colour as the letters in one-colour work.
Never
  • Stretch it, straighten the lean, or re-space the letters.
  • Add glows, gradients or extra drips.
  • Retype it in a font. Always use the files in logo/.
  • Put two logos on one side of a garment.
Keep clear of Carhartt

Your references are mostly Carhartt, so UNRESTD has to look like its own brand next to them. Don't use a square chest label or a C-shaped emblem, and don't borrow Carhartt's product names. The UNRESTD tab is wide and rectangular, and the brand's signal is the graffiti tag with its brass drip.

05LABELS

Labels & packaging

Small brands look big when the details are finished. Every jacket ships with a woven neck label, a care label, a numbered lot label and a kraft hang tag that tells the name story. It arrives in a black mailer with a slap sticker inside. The care label carries what US law requires on clothing: fibre content, country of origin, your RN number (a free registration from the FTC) and care instructions. The factory fills in the country of origin.

Artwork files are in labels/. Woven labels start at about $43 for 5 pieces (Super Label Store), so get a quote for 100 of each.

07THE MONEY

How this makes money

The brand earns from five streams. Start them in this order: the first two from day one, the third once you have a sample to show, and the fourth only to raise cash for the first batch.

  1. Main engine
    Jacket pre-orders

    Each Shift Jacket keeps about $87 to $122 after making costs and card fees. Customers pay before you pay the factory, so the batch pays for itself.

  2. Every day
    Print-on-demand basics

    Tees, the zip hoodie and the beanie keep about $17 to $54 each with no stock. They sell all year, and they fund your content and ads.

  3. Paid up front
    Crew orders

    Sell jackets to local businesses: barbershops, restaurants, tattoo shops, trade crews. Put their logo on the chest and your tab under it. 20 jackets at $140 each keeps about $1,250 to $1,950, and you charge extra for their embroidery. Crews reorder every winter.

  4. Starter cash
    Vintage flips

    Your references are full of vintage work jackets, and genuine vintage Carhartt J97 Detroit jackets are listed on eBay for $400 to $550. Thrift stores and estate sales sometimes turn them up for far less. Resell genuine pieces as they are, under your own seller name, and put the profit into lot 001. Never add your tab to another brand's jacket.

  5. Later
    Shoppable content

    TikTok Shop and Instagram product tags let the “While you slept” videos sell directly, so every post can take orders.

What it costs

Why the jackets can't be print-on-demand: print-on-demand services don't carry duck canvas work jackets. You can buy blank canvas jackets wholesale from workwear makers like Berne and DRI DUCK, but they come with the maker's own branding and cost $65 to $89 each, which leaves no room for your margin. So the jackets are made to your design by a small-batch factory.

08LAUNCH

The first 90 days

  1. Weeks 1–228 Sep – 11 Oct

    Name, factories, samples

    Buy unrestd.com today. Run the USPTO search, with extra care because of Unrest Clothing, and claim the handles. Send these drawings and specs to three low-minimum factories and ask each for a sample price, a per-jacket price at 40 and 100 units, and lead times. Order the print-on-demand samples and the woven tabs.

  2. Weeks 3–512 Oct – 1 Nov

    Wear-test the samples

    Samples usually take 2 to 4 weeks. Wear each jacket through a week of real shifts, and wash the basics five times. Fix the fit, then shoot every piece at the hours the brand is about: 4 a.m. loading docks, night kitchens, the drive home at dawn.

  3. Week 62 – 8 Nov

    Doors open

    The basics go on sale and jacket pre-orders open, with 24 hours of early access for the waitlist. Every jacket gets a lot number (001 to 040), and the product page shows the ship-by date.

  4. Weeks 7–129 Nov – 20 Dec

    Make the batch

    Place the batch order when pre-orders reach about 25. Bulk production takes 4 to 8 weeks. Run a content series called “While you slept”: short clips of real people working the night hours, filmed at the time they happen. Push the tee and beanie as holiday gifts.

  5. Week 13+late Dec – Jan

    Ship, then Drop 02

    Ship the numbered jackets and ask every buyer for a photo. Drop 02 is a cropped canvas trucker jacket (button front, two chest pockets, washed black), plus carpenter pants and cargos in Moss and black. Build it around what sold.

Goals to aim for
  • 300 waitlist sign-ups before doors open
  • 25 jacket pre-orders in the first two weeks, which triggers the batch
  • Under 3% of orders with a return or quality complaint
  • The sell-through of each colour, which decides batch two
Legal and pre-order basics
  • Register an LLC, get an EIN and turn on sales tax in Shopify.
  • File the trademark once the search is clear.
  • Show a clear ship-by date on every pre-order. The FTC's mail-order rule requires you to ship when you say you will, or to tell buyers about a delay and let them cancel.
  • Your own jackets need sewn-in labels showing the fibre content, the country of origin, care instructions and your brand name or an FTC registration number (RN).
